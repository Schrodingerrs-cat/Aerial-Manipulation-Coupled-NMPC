# Audit of AM3 baseline B and coupled NMPC N — 30 October 2026

## Verdict

**Not ready for a clean sign-off.** The core coupled plant and nominal NMPC have substantial supporting evidence. The baseline is locally unstable at stationary hover, the comparison does not use identical contact-detection timing, reference derivatives are inconsistent during path-progress ramps, and acceptance has material gaps. N passes the recorded nominal functional gate but fails the recorded enforced-deadline trials.

No evidence was found in the inspected B/N execution path of scripted pose tracking, hidden stabilising forces, disabling physical coupling for B, or substituting reference positions for simulated positions. That is not a certification that no possible shortcut exists. The problems below include confirmed defects, overstated validation, and explicitly assumed hardware/contact properties. They do not establish intentional falsification.

**Scope:** B and N only, as clarified by the user during this audit. Dynamics, geometry, task logic, numerical formulation, actuation, sensors, evaluation, provenance, tests and media are included. Other controllers are excluded from the findings and recommendations. Preliminary diagnostics outside B/N were excluded from this report and its numerical results after the scope clarification.

No production code, model, configuration, experiment result or running workload was modified. Additions are confined to `Findings/Audits/`. No solver compilation, controller tuning, training, or full gate rerun was performed.

## What B and N actually control

Both use the same 1.52 kg MuJoCo floating-base assembly, three active arm joints and a fixed wrist, physical joint torque sources, four rotor thrust actuators, motor lag, native wall contact and friction. B does not run on a physically decoupled plant.

| Item | B | N |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle control | Position PID, geometric attitude PD+I | Joint optimisation of vehicle and arm commands |
| Arm control | Position-only IK, joint PD, arm feedforward | Same seven-input NMPC optimisation |
| Dynamic model used | Correct total mass, fixed nominal COM/inertia, separate arm model | Full 9×9 coupled mass matrix and velocity/gravity terms |
| Contact handling | Shared task; nominal force inferred from indentation fed to thrust and joint feedforward | Shared task; predicted normal force and friction in dynamics |
| Rate | 200 Hz | 50 Hz |
| Imposed command latency | 5 ms | 20 ms |
| Actuator commands | Four rotor speeds, three joint torques | Same |
| Physical plant | Fully coupled | Fully coupled |

Thus B/N is a practical architecture/model/rate/delay comparison. It is not an isolated experiment changing only one coupling matrix block. B has substantial compensation already; it is not “uncontrolled.”

## Findings

### B01 — High: B develops oscillation with a stationary arm reference

**Sources:** `Code/ocrl/am3_ctrl_b.py:14`, `Code/scripts/am3_b_linear.py`, audit `checks.json`.

I ran B against the actual MuJoCo plant for ten seconds with the initial end-effector target held constant, default sensor noise, seed zero, no wall contact and no commanded arm trajectory. The arm remains actively controlled; it is not mechanically locked.

| Time window | Maximum tilt | Maximum base-position error |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0–1 s | 0.112° | 3.169 mm |
| 1–3 s | 0.412° | 3.384 mm |
| 3–6 s | 1.294° | 2.900 mm |
| 6–10 s | 8.955° | 16.476 mm |

The existing finite-difference closed-loop analysis gives spectral radius **1.002255357 per 5 ms**, with equilibrium residual 4.1e-12. That local model uses the full analytic predictor as its plant; the MuJoCo test independently demonstrates growing oscillation.

**Impact:** B's visible instability cannot be attributed exclusively to deliberate arm movement or wall interaction. Completion of finite episodes does not establish stable hover. The description “stable on every scenario” in the gate narrative overstates what completion shows.

**Required correction/evidence:** establish locally stable stationary hover, finite-perturbation recovery, noisy hover and final settling over representative arm postures before promoting the moving-arm comparison. Do not deliberately degrade B or insist that it crash.

### B02 — High: contact detection is four times faster for B

**Sources:** `am3_task.py:36`, `am3_episode.py:40`, `am3_task_v1.json`.

Task acquisition/loss thresholds are sample counts, advanced at each controller's own period. Independently reproduced:

| Event | B, 5 ms | N, 20 ms |
|---|---:|---:|
| Three samples to acquire | 15 ms | 60 ms |
| Five samples to lose contact | 25 ms | 100 ms |

Using the same code and JSON does not produce the same physical contact policy. Acquisition/recovery timing changes the applied references and the measured contact losses.

**Required:** elapsed-time thresholds or a common task clock; preserve explicitly declared rate differences and retest both controllers.

### B03 — High: commanded path velocity does not equal the derivative of commanded position

**Source:** `am3_task.py:55` and `:84`.

Path time advances as `tau_p += dt * rho`, but the returned tangential velocity omits `rho`. At a reproduced trace state with `rho=0.3`, the reference advertises **7.178 mm/s** while finite differences of reference position give **2.153 mm/s**.

B uses that velocity and its derivative in arm feedforward; N penalises the inconsistent velocity reference. During startup and recovery, both see contradictory commands, with different consequences.

**Required:** apply the chain rule to velocity and acceleration, including the derivative of progress rate; finite-difference test phase transitions and recovery. Steady-path derivatives alone are insufficient.

### B04 — High: N fails the measured deadline policy despite its nominal gate

**Sources:** `Code/results/am3_timing/summary.json`, `scripts/am3_timing.py`, `am3_episode.py:44`.

The saved nominal gate has N 30/30 functional successes. The subsequent deadline-enforced runs report:

| Path | Result |
|---|---|
| Line | Loss-of-control failsafe at 5.40 s |
| Circle | Loss-of-control failsafe at 5.40 s |
| Rounded triangle | Loss-of-control failsafe at 5.36 s |

The free-running line has cycle median **15.01 ms**, p99 **35.21 ms**, maximum **108.75 ms**, and **1.68%** of cycles exceeding 20 ms. These are inspected historical results, not new timing measurements during the audit.

The policy discards late commands and holds the previous command. It does not implement asynchronous late-result application or processor queuing; failure applies to this policy. Random independent skip tests do not replace testing phase-clustered measured overruns.

Timing starts after sensor generation and task advance. It includes the controller wrapper/reference preview/solver but is not the entire control-system execution budget. No real-time compliance claim is justified.

### B05 — High: the free-arm gate omits end-effector tracking

**Source:** `am3_eval.py:37`–44.

Tracking criteria are added only if the metric dictionary contains `trace`. Free-arm runs have no such dictionary. I took an otherwise successful saved free-arm metric record, changed its reported hand RMS error to **1000 mm** and maximum to **2000 mm**, and the evaluator still returned **all=True**.

This is a confirmed scoring defect, not evidence that N actually had metre-scale error. Its recorded accuracy is much better. It means 30/30 does not establish every intended task property.

**Required:** task-specific required fields and free-arm tracking thresholds; wall tasks must fail closed on missing trace/contact metrics. Re-score preserved data after correcting the evaluator.

### B06 — Medium/high: “continuous contact” and completion are weaker than the goal suggests

**Sources:** `am3_eval.py:26`–43, `am3_task.py:64`–73.

Contact-valid fraction selects settle/trace/endhold phases and excludes recovery. Up to two recoveries are accepted. Concatenating selected contact samples can also join intervals when computing longest uninterrupted contact. These definitions can hide meaningful detachment time from the valid-fraction denominator.

Task completion is based on the reference state machine. Retreat finishes when the reference gap reaches its target, rather than after measured clearance is confirmed. Endpoint uses tangential y/z error, and there is no independent measured retreat-gap acceptance criterion. The runner continues approximately one second after done, contrary to the plan's instruction to stop commands at done.

**Required:** report whole-interval contact losses and durations, clearly distinguish recovery-capable execution from uninterrupted contact, verify measured release/retreat, and establish the intended final settling/stopping policy explicitly.

### B07 — Medium/high: numerical validation does not establish production-step accuracy

**Sources:** `am3_check_rollout.py`, `am3_calibrate_contact.py`, `am3_nmpc.py:27`–32.

Production N integrates a 20 ms interval with four RK4 substeps: **5 ms per substep**. The saved rollout validation uses **0.5 ms substeps**, and the contact calibration prediction uses **1 ms steps**. Those results do not validate production discretisation.

The saved half-second rollout has about **1 mm position** and **0.0087 rad joint-angle** error. Machine-precision instantaneous dynamics agreement must not be described as machine-precision trajectory agreement. Contact calibration covers one posture and three closing speeds, not the full workspace, sliding/re-impact domain, or maximum-force limit.

**Required:** validate the exact production map and derivatives against finer integration, especially near contact; compare peak force, impulse, gap and state errors. Independently refine the plant timestep. Do not claim a bounded peak-force error from a few successful cases.

### B08 — Medium/high: IPOPT/acados parity does not cover the claimed trace/contact cases

**Sources:** `scripts/am3_check_parity.py`, `results/am3_predictor/solver_parity.json`, `am3_nmpc.py:108`.

The test advances a task using zero measured force while keeping the physical state at its initial free-hover state. Its result named `trace` actually reports `phase: approach`. The identical-trajectory cost calculation omits slack penalties; no full active-slack equivalence is established by that calculation.

The saved hover IPOPT cost is −0.0017, versus near zero for acados, so blanket 1e-9 cost agreement is not what the file says. Numerical lower-bound relaxation is a plausible explanation for a slightly negative theoretically nonnegative objective; it needs explicit treatment.

The acados wrapper accepts non-RTI iteration-limit status 2 as `ok`; the controller drops the `truncated` distinction. This affects reference-solver validation even though production N uses RTI. RTI status zero is also not a certificate of nonlinear convergence.

**Required:** parity tests at actual acquired-contact and trace states, deliberately violated constraints, terminal slacks, and derivative checks. Record solver status, residuals and truncation separately.

### B09 — Medium: solver diagnostics omit material details

**Sources:** `am3_ctrl_n.py:28`–35, `am3_nmpc.py:107`, `scripts/am3_run.py`.

When a first solve fails and a retry succeeds, the wrapper updates status/ok but keeps first-attempt solve time, slack and iteration fields. Failed-attempt counts are therefore not reconstructible from the aggregate failure field alone. Terminal slack is excluded from the `slack_max` collection. Per-step info, missed flags and slack diagnostics are not persisted in `log.npz`, despite being held in memory.

The episode's outer wall-clock check cannot interrupt a solver stuck inside a native call; it runs only after the call returns.

**Required:** persist each solve attempt and terminal/state constraint violations with units, plus external per-run watchdogs for native hangs. Preserve partial data on timeout.

### B10 — Medium: B tuning evidence contains ineffective parameters

**Sources:** `scripts/am3_b_linear.py`, `ocrl/am3_ctrl_b.py:27` and `:47`.

The linear scan includes `w_joint`, `zeta_joint` and `t_lat`, but current B uses `kp_joint`, `kd_joint` and a fixed `self.t_lat=1.5*period`. Those scan knobs do not vary all the dynamics the scan labels imply. Older results may have used a different implementation; establish that provenance rather than counting trials as sufficient tuning evidence.

B's lag inversion amplifies the desired-minus-predicted rotor speed by about **17.24** before clipping. It assumes 50 ms lag, whereas the plant has 40/60 ms. This is a plausible oscillation contributor, not an isolated proven cause. The baseline includes aggressive dynamic compensation and should not be portrayed as a generic untuned PID.

B also performs eight unbounded Newton IK steps without explicit operational-limit/singularity checks. Its task-space integral correction changes the target beyond the nominal prechecked path. A nominal feasibility scan does not cover all such corrected targets.

### B11 — Medium: B/N noise realisations and reference access are not fully matched

**Sources:** `am3_episode.Sensors`, `am3_ctrl_b.py`, `am3_ctrl_n.params`.

The same seed is consumed once per controller cycle. B consumes four times as many noise samples per second; equal seeds are not identical measurement histories at equal physical times. B uses current references and a short acceleration preview; N receives a 0.2 s horizon. The rate, latency and preview differences must remain explicit.

This does not make a practical architecture comparison invalid by itself, but it limits attribution. Use a shared time-indexed sensor process if paired noise histories are claimed, and separate practical performance from causal claims about coupling alone.

### B12 — Medium: actuator prediction and actuator scoring are incomplete

**Sources:** `am3_plant.servo_limit`, `am3_pred.py:53`, `am3_eval.py:31`–32.

The plant clips joint torque with a speed-dependent envelope; the predictor uses raw commanded torque. Under the intended soft 3 rad/s joint-rate bound, the 1 N·m cap normally dominates. Above about **5.37 rad/s**, the driving envelope becomes tighter. Because rate constraints are soft, prediction/plant disagreement is possible precisely during poor-control trajectories.

`cmd_clip_fraction` compares newly computed commands with delayed applied commands. Ordinary command changes are counted as clipping. Requested/applied torque must be compared at aligned physical times after envelope processing.

Rotor reserves use hardcoded nominal thrust coefficients and 0.1/10 N endpoints. That is adequate only for the stated nominal configuration; it does not measure actual reserve under propulsion mismatch. Physics rotor speeds are logged after motor integration while the interval's thrust used the pre-update speed. Log actual actuator forces as well.

### B13 — Medium: applied-input logs refer to the preceding interval

**Sources:** `am3_episode.py:52`–55.

The runner logs `u_app` before assigning the pending command that will drive the next physics interval. An independent marker-controller test gives:

| Time | Logged torque | Torque actually applied in following interval |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.02 s | 0 | 0.001 N·m |
| 0.04 s | 0.001 | 0.002 N·m |
| 0.06 s | 0.002 | 0.003 N·m |

The previous-interval value is meaningful if explicitly labelled that way. It is wrong to use it directly to predict `x[k+1]` from `x[k]`. This compromises replay/residual analyses that make that assumption. It does **not** mean the actual N command-latency queue is absent or that its closed-loop poses were fabricated.

**Required:** log previous-interval and next-interval command identities explicitly; check replay alignment against actual physics-step commands.

### B14 — Medium: collision/clearance assurance is partial

**Sources:** `am3_static.Rig.clearances`, `am3_nmpc._constraints`, feasibility scripts.

Clearance checks use native contact generation with margins, which still filters adjacent body pairs. That cannot certify all potentially colliding link surfaces. The NMPC checks two point heights, not full link geometry. Its height expression projects onto world up although comments describe clearance below the body-fixed rotor plane; those differ when tilted.

Path feasibility is sampled and uses a quasi-static attitude approximation. It computes joint derivatives with a base-frame Jacobian and world-frame reference velocities without the corresponding rotation, and omits changing attitude-rate effects. That is not full dynamic path-feasibility certification, even if numerical inverse-dynamics residuals are small for the imposed derivatives.

**Required:** correct frame accounting; document the approximation; independently check full geometry and actual trajectory extrema. Preserve sampled-path success as sampled evidence, not a workspace-wide guarantee.

### B15 — Medium: friction validation uses pad-centre speed rather than material-point speed

**Source:** `scripts/am3_check_friction.py`.

The script estimates tangential velocity from successive pad-centre positions. The contact model correctly uses the rotating sphere's material-point velocity, which also includes rotation. Friction opposes slip at the contact material point, not necessarily pad-centre translation. The saved median force/speed relationship cannot independently validate that rotational contribution or all sticking/reversal behaviour.

**Required:** compare native contact forces against contact-point material velocity, including rotation, at aligned physics times; test positive/negative sliding and transitions separately.

### B16 — Medium: paths are simpler than some descriptions imply

**Source:** `am3_path.rounded_polygon` and path configuration.

The “rounded_triangle” is an **open polyline** through three vertices with an interior circular fillet. It does not close a triangle. It is tangent-continuous but curvature jumps at line/arc joins, so acceleration can jump during nonzero-speed traversal. The code documents this, but reports and videos should not imply a closed, acceleration-smooth triangular contour.

This is not fabricated tracking: it is the actual implemented path. Define the intended curve and smoothness explicitly before generalising results to arbitrary trajectories.

### B17 — Medium: evaluator tests do not enforce their own stated expectations

**Source:** `scripts/am3_check_eval.py`.

The counterexample script produces dictionaries and writes them; it contains no assertions requiring each expected failure. A test process can exit successfully even if a counterexample unexpectedly passes. Deadline overrun remains a functional pass by design because timing is separate; this must be checked against a separate timing predicate, not implied to fail the same gate.

**Required:** explicit expected-outcome assertions, required-field counterexamples, free-arm tracking, recovery/contact denominators, actual retreat, and control-versus-physics sampling tests.

### B18 — Medium: provenance does not freeze the complete executable experiment

**Sources:** `scripts/am3_freeze.py`, `am3_run.py`, `am3_pred.py`.

The v1 manifest covers 22 package/config/model files but no runner, evaluator-driver, model-generator, tuning or recording scripts. The predictor imports `airframe_inertial` from a generator script outside that frozen file list. Generated libraries and solver dependencies are also not fully fingerprinted there. Per-run source hashes cover package Python only, not the complete experiment/configuration. The runner promises a resolved `config.json` but does not write it.

The gate script defaults to the mutable draft if no acceptance path is supplied. The saved B/N gate artifacts do explicitly name the frozen acceptance file, and the draft currently matches it; I found no threshold discrepancy in these saved results.

**Required:** preserve a complete dependency/config/scenario manifest per run, immutable versions and resolved command lines. Verify snapshots before and after batches.

### B19 — Medium: media does not meet the explicit screen-recording requirement

**Sources:** `am3_record.py`, `am3_compare.py`, `CLAUDE.md`, media README.

The current AM3 videos are genuine frames rendered from the live closed-loop MuJoCo instance, then encoded at 25 fps. They are not actual viewer screen captures, as explicitly required by the repository instructions. Simulated-time 1× playback is not wall-clock real-time execution.

The inspected circle frame shows the centred connected mount and compact hand pad, with no long rod. The rotors are stationary cylinder visuals. The trace uses real pad-centre positions rather than projecting them onto the wall, but the green path is on the wall while the pad centre is about 12 mm away: explain or consistently visualise contact surface versus centre. Trace colour uses true force, while some documentation calls it measured force. Frame scheduling can differ by one controller step; the inspected ten-second frame shows B at 10.00 s and N at 10.02 s.

**Required:** retain these as closed-loop render evidence; provide actual screen captures for the stated milestone and label time, controller, contact and geometry consistently.

### B20 — Medium: documentation overstates or lags the evidence

`controllers_v3.md`/STATUS describe N weights as essentially untuned, while the tuning script and gate narrative acknowledge development tuning and a validation-driven normal-position weight change. That validation set is no longer untouched validation. The held-out list remains separate: the audit found no exact nominal scenario duplicates between held-out and development/validation.

STATUS also contains old open/complete statements that conflict with existing artifacts. Hardware assumptions, nominal performance, timing failure, baseline instability and remaining model validation should be reported together rather than allowing an old “DONE” label to carry stronger meaning.

## Positive checks: what is supported

### Independent instantaneous dynamics

I reran the 50-state predictor-versus-MuJoCo checks without overwriting the project's stored output:

| Quantity | Maximum absolute difference |
|---|---:|
| Forward kinematics | 3.33e-16 m |
| Joint Jacobian | 2.81e-16 |
| Finite-difference Jacobian | 5.52e-11 |
| Full mass matrix | 1.00e-9 |
| Gravity/velocity bias | 1.08e-8 |
| Contact-point material velocity | 5.55e-16 |
| Acceleration | 5.44e-6, against sampled scale 1447 |

The mass matrix is symmetric and positive definite in the samples; base–joint cross blocks are nonzero. Eleven acceleration cases were skipped because they had contact. The test uses direct joint torques and moderate joint speeds, so it does not validate high-speed saturation or general contact dynamics. The negligible extra payload body also means exact machine-zero agreement should not be expected everywhere.

### Independent whole-system force and moment balance

Using native MuJoCo body COM Jacobians and inertias at zero velocity, I evaluated linear/angular momentum rates against rotor, gravity and contact loads, independently of the analytic predictor's acceleration routine.

1. At initial hover, force/moment residuals were about **2.2e-15 N** and **1.0e-15 N·m**.
2. Adding **0.05 N·m shoulder torque** with rotor commands unchanged produced body pitch acceleration **−29.075 rad/s²**, base acceleration approximately **[−0.0166, 0, 0.1583] m/s²**, while total COM acceleration remained essentially unchanged. Internal actuation moves the base and arm in reaction without creating net external force.
3. At a controlled 1 mm initial wall penetration, normal force was **0.571 N**, total COM x acceleration **−0.376 m/s²**, and body pitch acceleration **−1.926 rad/s²**. Force/moment balance again closed near machine precision, with no unintended contacts.

These are instantaneous physics diagnostics, not a promised closed-loop transient or real-hardware validation. They show that arm reaction and wall-force transmission are present. The drone base can initially accelerate in a different direction from the total COM because the arm is also moving; interpreting a video from base displacement alone can be misleading.

### Saved results cross-check

Re-scoring the saved tables using frozen criteria reproduces **N 30/30 and B 1/30**. For all 60 B/N held-out logs, independently recomputed maximum base error and physics-resolution peak force match their table values exactly. This supports those recorded numbers, but not omitted criteria or real-time success.

The nominal lists use different seeds; no identical scenario records were found across held-out versus development/validation. No new unseen-domain performance claim is made by this audit.

## Physical assumptions and limits

| Area | Current assumption / implementation | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Assembly | Supplied 1.52 kg primitive-geometry robot, centred mount, three active joints, wrist fixed at 20° | Measured physical robot inertias or structural compliance |
| Hand | 12 mm-radius contact pad included in unchanged 0.10 kg hand inertia | Independently measured redesigned hand mass/COM/inertia |
| Rotors | Sites ±0.10 m, radius 0.089 m; 0.1–10 N per rotor; 180–1800 rad/s; reaction arm 0.016 m | Tested propulsion map for the supplied drone |
| Motor response | First-order 40 ms up / 60 ms down | Full ESC, battery, aerodynamic or rotor gyroscopic model |
| Servos | XM430-class 3 N·m stall, 8.06 rad/s no-load; 1 N·m continuous assumed | Actual actuator thermal/continuous torque and bus dynamics |
| Contact | k=600 N/m, c=8 N·s/m, μ=0.15; native softness calibration; 10 μm predictor onset, 2 mm/s friction regularisation | Identified real wall/pad properties over the workspace |
| Sensing | Noisy truth state: 0.1 mm position, 0.01° attitude, 1 mm/s velocity, 10 mN force noise | A demonstrated deployable estimator with delays/bias/outliers |
| Task | Fixed base position; hand trajectories; 1 mm indentation reference, roughly 0.6 N static preload | Precision force regulation, arbitrary wall geometries or unlimited workspace |
| Aerodynamics | Rotor forces and reaction moments only | Downwash-on-arm, wall/ground effects, crossflow or flexible airframe behaviour |

The numerical consistency of a model and simulator does not establish that these assumed values describe a real robot. Choosing a lower-friction pad or a smaller feasible patch can be legitimate design work when declared and frozen; it is not evidence of universal robustness or identification from hardware. There is no basis here to call the assumed physical parameters measured research-platform specifications.

## Actual constraints

N has hard command bounds on rotor speed and joint torque. Its **soft, node-based** constraints cover 15° tilt, operational q1 ±35°, q2 −80° to −5°, q3 −125° to −30°, joint rates ±3 rad/s, predicted normal force ≤4 N, closing speed ≤0.04 m/s in approach/recover/position and 0.5 m/s otherwise, and elbow/wrist point-height surrogates. They are not hard guarantees on the physical state between nodes.

Mechanical XML limits are wider: q1 ±90°, q2 ±120°, q3 ±150°. Both controllers encounter the same physical plant limits, but B does not enforce the same operational state constraints internally.

Frozen functional scoring uses base max 20 mm, tilt max 15°, wall-tracking RMS 3 mm, endpoint 5 mm, 95% coverage within 5 mm, selected-phase contact fraction 95%, up to two recoveries, peak force 4 N, rotor reserve 0.3 N, completion, no unexpected contact and no fallback. The omissions discussed above mean that “all” is not yet equivalent to the complete plan. Timing is separate.

## Required order of work

1. Keep current B/N results and videos immutable. Label B's demonstrated stationary-hover instability and N's timing failures.
2. Repair and validate stationary B hover across relevant postures, noise and finite perturbations; check IK validity and actual actuator response. Do not manufacture a weak comparator.
3. Correct shared task timing, progress derivatives, interval-labelled command logs, scoring requirements and diagnostic persistence. Re-score existing logs where sufficient.
4. Validate the production NMPC integration and active-contact/slack formulation; complete physics-resolution constraints and geometric checks.
5. Resolve measured deadline bursts with a declared command scheduler, then test actual applied-delay behaviour. Random skip tolerance alone is insufficient.
6. Freeze a new complete B/N experiment and run a fresh disjoint gate. Supply the required screen captures with matched scenarios and clearly labelled simulation/wall-clock time.

**Sign-off condition:** convincing nominal N tracking already exists. A defensible B/N comparison, faithful physical assumptions, full task scoring and deployable timing have not all been established. No claim that everything is perfect, all corners are covered, or no shortcuts remain is justified.
