# Physics isolation: why does the drone move the way it does? (30 Oct 2026)

Question (user, 30 Oct): is the drone's motion a believable physical response to the arm moving and the hand pushing on the wall, or only a controller artefact? Method: MuJoCo's own whole-assembly Newton-Euler balance (`Code/ocrl/am3_probe.py`, built from MuJoCo subtree momentum and the actual rotor/contact/disturbance loads; it does **not** use the CasADi predictor)

    m a_COM = F_rotors + m g + F_wall        dL_COM/dt = M_rotors + r_wall x F_wall

Arm-drone reaction forces are internal and cannot appear in these equations. Scripts: `Code/scripts/am3_physics_check.py` (steps 1-3, open loop), `am3_physics_closed.py` (steps 1b, 4, 5), `am3_hover_check.py`. Raw numbers: `Code/results/am3_physics/*.json`; figures here: `step*.png`.

## What was established
**Step 1 - stationary arm, no wall, known loads** (`step1.png`). A 0.3 N force at the drone-body COM gives a_COM = 0.19747 m/s^2 against F/m = 0.19737 (ratio 1.0005); a 0.02 N m torque gives dL_COM/dt = 0.02004 N m (ratio 1.002). Newton and moment residuals stay below 5e-5 N / 3e-6 N m. The effective pitch inertia about the COM with the joints torque-held (free) is **0.0027 kg m^2**, versus 0.0167 kg m^2 for the locked arm posture that baseline B's attitude loop uses (see limits below).

**Step 1b - stable hover feedback, same pulses** (`step1b.png`). Both controllers see the same physical onset (first 10 ms of the force pulse: B 0.1972, N 0.2096 m/s^2 against 0.1974; N's 6 % excess is within its 0.2 deg noise-driven tilt, i.e. about 0.03 m/s^2). Recovery after the 0.3 N pulse: B peak 8.9 mm / 1.2 deg, N 4.0 mm / 2.0 deg; after the torque pulse both about 2.2 mm; base error in the last 2 s: B 0.92 mm, N 0.25 mm.

**Step 2 - one joint at a time, rotors fixed at trim** (`step2.png`). A 0.05 N m joint pulse accelerates the joint (q1 35, q2 51, q3 29 rad/s^2) and, over the first 8 ms, the **base** with exactly the coupling term -M_bb^-1 M_bq qdd (base pitch accel q2 pulse: measured -27.666, predicted -27.658 rad/s^2; q3: 10.568 vs 10.561; yaw from q1: -14.08 vs -14.09), while the COM acceleration is 0.0007-0.003 m/s^2 against 0.05-0.2 m/s^2 of base acceleration. Rotor speeds do not change (open loop). Later the COM follows the rotor thrust that rotates with the tilting base (dots in the plot), and the balance residual stays below 1.3e-3 N / 2.6e-4 N m. So: arm acceleration reacts on the base (internal), not on the total COM.

**Step 3 - gentle wall contact, no sliding** (`step3.png`). Approach at 2 cm/s from 2 mm, rotors and joint torques at the free-flight trim: peak Fn 0.199 N, wall moment about the COM up to 0.031 N m, COM x acceleration equals (F_rotors + F_wall)/m to 4e-6 m/s^2, moment residual 2e-7 N m. The base pitches and the arm swings (joints are torque-held, not locked), so pitch acceleration is not simply wall moment / one inertia.

**Step 4 - sliding contact, closed loop** (`step4_line.png`, `step4_circle.png`). During the trace the balance closes to 1.7e-6 N (B) and 5.7e-5 N (N). Steady state: the steady tilt (2-2.5 deg, held by both controllers) tilts the thrust vector and so supplies the horizontal force that balances the wall force, while the rotor moment about y (differential rotor thrust) balances the wall moment (about 60 mN m for Fn 0.54 N at the 0.28 m lever); these are two different mechanisms, not one. Friction/normal ratio 0.11-0.14 (mu = 0.15; lower values during stick and reversal). Lateral balance: rms(F_rotor,y + friction_y) equals m a_COM,y exactly; it is 0.075 N (B) and 0.023 N (N) on the line, against a friction force of about 0.064-0.077 N, so B's lateral drift (base 25 mm, COM 31 mm) is a lateral rotor force that does not cancel friction by about the size of the friction itself, slowly oscillating, not the friction acting alone. For N the COM moves 8.8 mm (line) / 5.5 mm (circle) in y because the arm moves, while the base moves only 0.3-0.4 mm: base and COM displacements differ, as the plan warned.

**Step 5 - fixed-command diagnostic** (`step5.png`). Rotors frozen at the hover trim and N's joint-torque schedule replayed open loop: base deviation 0.38 mm at 0.5 s, 4.3 mm at 1.0 s, 12.8 mm at 1.25 s, 77 mm at 1.5 s (tilt 25 deg); with the joint torques frozen as well (control) it stays below 0.002 mm / 0.0003 deg. In the window 1.0-1.5 s the measured base angular acceleration follows the coupling term (correlation 0.89 roll, 0.84 pitch; yaw -0.22, not explained). B and N closed loop on the same arm motion: base max in the first 2 s 42 mm and 0.28 mm.

**Stationary hover** (`Code/results/am3_physics/hover_check_*.json`). B v4 and N hold a stationary arm at four postures for 30 s with sensor noise (base 0.2-0.4 mm and 0.2-0.3 mm in the last 10 s) and recover from a 0.05 m/s / 0.15 rad/s kick. **The earlier B (v3, since discarded) did not**: it lost control after 8.5-16.9 s at the three hover postures, growing tilt exactly as in audit finding B01, linear spectral radius 1.0015-1.0023 per 5 ms. B was re-tuned with long stationary hover as a requirement (B v4, `Findings/Codebase/controllers_v3.md`); this lowered its moving-arm accuracy (development base RMS 11-46 mm).

## What this does and does not show
* The wall force and the arm's reaction are transmitted exactly as rigid-body physics requires; contact does not by itself cause oscillation: in open loop and with N it produces a smooth tilt (about 2 deg) and a steady rotor-moment demand.
* Whether a base motion comes from the arm reaction, the COM shift, the wall moment, friction or the controller's own feedback can be read from the balance terms above for each moment in time. Base displacement alone is misleading: base and COM move differently.
* Not shown: that B's residual error is *caused* by ignoring the coupling. B's attitude loop uses the locked-posture inertia (6x the effective value with free joints, step 1), which is a plausible contributor; this was not isolated by a controlled experiment (that would need a B variant with the coupling terms added, which is outside the requested B-versus-N scope).
* The step-5 replay uses N's torque schedule, which contains N's own noise-driven corrections; because the open-loop assembly is unstable, small schedule differences are amplified, so it shows the magnitude of the uncorrected sensitivity, not a clean linear response.
* Predictor-versus-MuJoCo agreement is not used as evidence here; the probe uses MuJoCo only. Parameters (inertias, rotor map, contact stiffness and friction) remain assumptions.
